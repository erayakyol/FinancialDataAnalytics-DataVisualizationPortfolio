Leveraged home-equity return explorer

How the two lines are computed. Prices are the BIS real residential property price index (inflation-adjusted, 2010 = 100), so both multiples are in real terms. The home is bought in the first quarter of the purchase year at price P0; the buyer pays the down payment (his own money, 1 − loan-to-value) × P0 and borrows the rest with an interest-only mortgage at the chosen constant rate. Unlevered equity multiple = Pt / P0: the cash buyer's wealth per unit of money invested. Levered equity multiple = (Pt − loan − interest paid so far) / down payment: the mortgaged buyer's wealth per unit of his own money. Below zero the loan plus the interest paid exceed the home's value: negative equity, the down payment is lost in full.

Simplifications, stated so that the numbers are not over-read. No taxes, no transaction costs, no amortisation (the loan stays constant), no rent yield or rent saved (carry = rent yield − mortgage rate is not modelled, so the levered line is a conservative lower bound for an owner-occupier who would otherwise pay rent), a constant mortgage rate over the whole holding period, and a real price index combined with a nominal mortgage rate (which overstates the real interest cost by the inflation rate times the loan). The default rate is the latest Swiss mean rate on variable mortgages published by the Swiss National Bank; for other countries set the slider to a local rate.
